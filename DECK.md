## Slavery & Black America, 1790–1860

### Cotton & the Slave South

*1793–1860.* How cotton and the domestic slave trade built the Deep South, and how white Southerners defended slavery.

| Term | Definition | Yawp |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cotton gin (1793) | Eli Whitney’s machine for separating seeds from short-staple cotton, which made inland cotton profitable and spread slavery across the Deep South. | 8.III, 11.II |

| 4.3:Bank of the United States | Says the bank was “chartered 1811 and again in 1816.” The First Bank was chartered in 1791 and its charter expired in 1811; the Second Bank was chartered in 1816. |
| 4.8:1832 Jefferson dinner toasts | The Jackson–Calhoun toasts were at the Jefferson Day dinner of April 1830, not 1832. |
| 4.4:Treaty of Fort Jackson | Gives “about 14 million acres”; the figure is usually cited as about 21–23 million. The card describes the cession without a number. |
| 4.5:Erie Canal (1825) | Gives 350 miles; the canal is usually given as 363 miles. The card says “about 360 miles.” |
| 4.5:Steamboat New Orleans (1811–1812) | Says the boat ran “to New Orleans and back”; it is usually described as never returning upriver to Pittsburgh. The card says it ran from Pittsburgh down to New Orleans. |
| 4.5:Eli Whitney / cotton gin (1794) | 1794 is the patent year; Whitney built the gin in 1793. The card uses 1793. |
| 4.11:1840 split / Abby Kelly | Her name is usually spelled Abby Kelley. The card uses Kelley and accepts either spelling in Type mode. |
| 4.4:First Seminole War (1818) | The war is usually dated 1817–1818; Jackson’s invasion of Florida was in 1818. The card uses 1817–1818. |
